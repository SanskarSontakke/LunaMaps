# Deep zoom by streaming: an `ortho` layer from the Kaguya TC mosaic

Date: 2026-10-08. Follows amendment 2 §6. Status: **design**, not started.

## Why this exists

`wms_basemaps3/Moon/Kaguya_TCortho_Mosaic_Global_4096ppd_cog.tif` is 7.4 m/px, global,
single-band, **tiled 512 / JPEG / 9 overviews**, on `GCS_Moon` R = 1,737,400 with bounds
exactly (-180, -90, 180, 90) — the same equirectangular grid contract `scripts/grid.py`
already pins. Verified by streaming, not by catalog text: a 2048² window over Tycho came
back in 5.1 s with 1.9× the Laplacian variance of WAC upsampled to the same footprint
(131.8 against 61.9).

So L8 (41.6 m/px), L9 (20.8) and L10 (10.4) are all **real data**; L11 (5.2) is where
interpolation starts. Global L10 is impossible for a local-first app (L0..L10 is 2,796,202
tiles), so the depth stays regional — but regional at L10, not L8.

## The constraint that decides the shape

Kaguya TC is a **shaded orthophoto**: sun-illuminated, not illumination-normalized.
Amendment 2 §2 item 4 bans mixing two shaded sources, and Gate G2's proof (mareMedian
33.5 / highlandMedian 80.5 / contrast 47.0) is measured on WAC albedo. Therefore Kaguya
may not feed `paper`, may not feed the `photo` luminance term, and may not be blended into
either at a level boundary. It gets its own layer, labelled for what it is — the same
ruling D7 made for GLD100, applied to a source that is 16× finer.

## Design

**Layer `ortho`.** Chip label "Orthophoto", gloss "Kaguya TC shaded orthophoto, 7.4 m/px —
shaded relief, not albedo; deep zoom inside the marked regions only". Levels 7–10, and
**only inside ~10 site boxes**. There is deliberately no global `ortho` pyramid: streaming
L0–L7 would be a second 3.7 GB bake for a layer whose entire value is what you see at
10 m/px.

**Regions.** `public/data/regions.json`, generated by `scripts/regions.py` from the held
gazetteer plus a curated list, each entry `{name, kind, box: [w,s,e,n], maxLevel}`. First
set: the six Apollo and three Surveyor landing sites, Chang'e 5, plus Tycho, Copernicus,
Aristarchus, Hadley Rille, Rümker, Orientale and the south pole. Measured by `scripts/regions.py` on 2026-10-08, not estimated: **30,172 tiles**
across L8–L10 for the 13 regions it ships — and the estimate above was wrong in a
way worth keeping, because the south polar cap alone is 23,040 of them (76 %). The
sites are cheap (22–29 tiles each at 0.5°); Orientale is 4,430; the cap is what a
6° band of every longitude costs. If that is too much, the knob is the cap's
latitude edge, not the site boxes.

*Amended 2026-10-09, after both polar sources were measured: the knob was used and the
cap is gone.* Amendment §7 has the coverage sweep, the grid's 20.8 m/px latitude ceiling
at L9 and the spoke-to-bbox read amplification. The plan is now **12 regions and 7,132
tiles** (7,106 distinct — apollo-15's box lies inside rima-hadley's and shares 26 tiles),
and every one of them is baked: `test_disk_guard.py` asserts plan == delivery, so the
dead-weight cost estimate this paragraph carried for a day cannot come back silently.

**Streaming, not downloading.** `scripts/stream_bake.py` opens the COG through
`/vsicurl/` and reads each box **once at native resolution in row chunks**, then slices
tiles in memory. That is ~10 large sequential reads (~210 MB each) instead of ~62,000
block requests. The alternative — one window per tile — was measured to fail outright
under load, so the reader needs a bounded retry with backoff before it can be trusted at
all, and bulk-per-box avoids most of the exposure anyway.

**Availability, because Cesium does not know about holes.** `UrlTemplateImageryProvider`
has no per-tile availability hook, so `ortho` needs a thin custom provider implementing
`TileImageryProvider.requestImage` plus `availableTiles(rectangle, level)` backed by the
manifest. The manifest gains, per layer, a compact `availability` list of
`{level, ranges: [[x0,x1,y0,y1], ...]}` — ranges, not tile lists, so it stays small enough
to fetch with the rest of the manifest. `src/lib/assets.ts` refuses a `top` the grid does
not define, and now also refuses an availability entry outside `[0, top]`.

**The empty case is a feature, not a bug.** Selecting `ortho` outside a box shows the
existing notice path with the region list, and picking a region flies the camera there —
which is D11's region overlay doing its job. An app that silently shows a black globe for
a layer the user can select is worse than one that says where the data is.

## Order of work

1. `scripts/regions.py` + `regions.json`, and the manifest availability schema. Unit tests
   first; no network.
2. `scripts/stream_bake.py`: box reader with bounded retry, tile slicing at L7–L10,
   `wrap_x=False` for boxes (amendment 2 §2 item 3 — a site box has real margins, so the
   periodic blur must not be used), JPEG encode, resume.
3. The custom provider in `src/lib/cesium/`, driven by the manifest, with a test that a
   tile outside every box is answered from availability and never requested.
4. Run it for real after the L7 bake finishes, then Gate G1-style proof: a known L10 tile
   compared against a hand-computed window of the source.

## Open questions this plan does not answer

- Whether 6°×6° is the right box. It is 1,100 km wide at the equator; the Apollo sites are
  ~10 km science targets, so a box 60× smaller would be defensible and 3,600× cheaper.
- Whether `ortho` should cross-fade into anything at its L7 boundary or simply not exist
  below it. Current answer: not exist, and let the chip say so.
